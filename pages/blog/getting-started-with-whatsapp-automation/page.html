WhatsApp automation for beginners: what to automate first

Chatberry Team · 19 Oct 2026 · 5 min read

On this page
  1. Start with the questions you answer every day
  2. Five journeys worth automating first
  3. Templates, sessions and what they cost
  4. Keep a person one tap away
  5. Measure outcomes, not messages
  6. Your next step

Most businesses don’t need a huge automation project. They need the five or six conversations they have every day to happen faster and more reliably. This guide shows where to start.

Start with the questions you answer every day

Open your WhatsApp chats from last week and count. You’ll find the same handful of messages again and again: prices, timings, location, order status, booking changes. These are your first automation candidates, because the answer rarely changes and customers want it immediately.

A good first automation has three qualities:

  • It happens often, at least a few times a day.
  • The answer comes from information you already have, like a price list, a schedule or an order system.
  • A slow reply costs you something: a missed booking, a cold lead or an annoyed customer.

Five journeys worth automating first

  1. Instant first reply. Greet every new enquiry within seconds, ask one useful question, and route it to the right person.
  2. Appointment booking and reminders. Let people pick a slot, then remind them the day before. No-shows drop noticeably when reminders arrive on WhatsApp.
  3. Order and delivery updates. Confirm the order, share dispatch details and tell customers when it’s arriving.
  4. Payment reminders. A polite nudge with a payment link works better than a phone call for most small dues.
  5. Feedback after service. Ask for a rating while the experience is fresh, and send unhappy customers straight to a person.

Templates, sessions and what they cost

On the official WhatsApp Business Platform, there are two kinds of conversations. When a customer messages you, a 24-hour customer service window opens and you can reply freely. When you start the conversation, for example with a reminder or an offer, you must use a template that WhatsApp has approved.

CategoryTypical useTemplate needed?
MarketingOffers, launches, re-engagementYes
UtilityOrder updates, reminders, confirmationsYes
AuthenticationOne-time passwordsYes
ServiceReplies inside the 24-hour windowNo

Meta charges differently for each category and updates its rates from time to time, so check the current WhatsApp pricing page before you plan a large campaign.

Keep a person one tap away

Automation should make your team faster, not hide them. Every journey needs a clear way out: a “talk to someone” button, or a rule that hands the chat to a person when the customer is upset or the question is unusual. Customers forgive a bot that can’t help. They don’t forgive one that won’t let them reach a human.

Measure outcomes, not messages

Sending more messages isn’t the goal. Pick one number per journey and watch it for a month:

  • First reply time for new enquiries.
  • No-show rate for appointments.
  • Days to collect payments.
  • Share of chats resolved without a person.

If the number improves, add the next journey. If it doesn’t, look at where people drop off and fix that step first.

Your next step

Pick one journey from the list above and set it up this week. When you’re ready to try it on your own number, get started with Chatberry or book a demo on WhatsApp and we’ll help you build it.
